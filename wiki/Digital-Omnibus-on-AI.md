# Digital Omnibus on AI

The **Digital Omnibus on AI** is a European Commission proposal to amend the EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689). It is part of the broader Digital Omnibus package tabled by the Commission on **19 November 2025**, which also contains a separate proposal amending the GDPR, ePrivacy Directive, NIS2 Directive and Data Act. The two proposals are formally distinct legislative instruments. This article focuses on the **AI Omnibus** (sometimes referred to as Omnibus VII).

## Purpose

The Commission's stated aim is to:
- Grant additional compliance time for high-risk Annex III obligations and Article 50 transparency, in light of delayed harmonised standards and incomplete national implementation.
- Streamline some procedural and reporting requirements without altering the AI Act's core risk-based architecture.
- Add a targeted new prohibition (the ninth Article 5 prohibition) covering AI generation of non-consensual intimate content and CSAM, in response to civil-society and Member State pressure.

## Provisional agreement reached on 7 May 2026

On **7 May 2026**, the Council of the EU and the European Parliament reached a **provisional political agreement** on the AI Omnibus, after the first political trilogue on 28 April 2026 ended without agreement following roughly 12 hours of negotiation.

The 7 May agreement is **not yet final**. It must still be:
1. Endorsed by the Council and the European Parliament.
2. Subjected to legal-linguistic revision.
3. Published in the Official Journal of the EU.

**Until OJ publication, the original Regulation 2024/1689 dates remain legally binding.** This means 2 August 2026 remains the binding date for Annex III high-risk obligations, Article 50 transparency, and the Article 57 sandbox deadline. Once the Omnibus is published, those dates shift as set out below.

## Substantive changes in the 7 May 2026 text

| Provision | Original AI Act (2024/1689) | Digital Omnibus on AI (provisional 7 May 2026) |
|---|---|---|
| **High-risk Annex III obligations** | Apply from 2 August 2026 | Apply from **2 December 2027** |
| **High-risk in regulated products (Annex I / Art. 6(1))** | Apply from 2 August 2027 | Apply from **2 August 2028** |
| **Article 50 transparency obligations** | Apply from 2 August 2026 | Apply from **2 December 2026** (3-month grace period from OJ publication, reduced from the 6 months originally proposed) |
| **Article 57 sandbox deadline** | 2 August 2026 | **2 August 2027** (postponed by one year) |
| **Article 5 prohibitions** | Eight prohibitions, in force since 2 February 2025 | A **ninth prohibition** is added, covering AI generation of non-consensual sexual or intimate content depicting identifiable persons and child sexual abuse material (CSAM). Same Tier 1 penalty (€35M / 7% of global turnover). |
| **Article 4 AI literacy** | In force since 2 February 2025 | Unchanged |
| **GPAI obligations (Articles 51–55)** | In force since 2 August 2025 | Unchanged |

## EDPB-EDPS Joint Opinion 1/2026

The European Data Protection Board and the European Data Protection Supervisor jointly published Opinion 1/2026 on **20 January 2026**, raising concerns that several simplification measures in the Omnibus risk weakening individual rights, in particular around:
- Lawful basis for AI training
- Special-category-data exceptions under GDPR Article 9
- The interaction between Article 22 GDPR and AI Act human-oversight duties

Source: https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2026-01/edpb_edps_jointopinion_202601_proposal_ai-omnibus_en.pdf

The Opinion is influential and signals where DPAs are likely to focus enforcement attention once the Omnibus is in force.

## Practical implications

- **Plan for the earlier date.** The honest planning rule is unchanged: build for the original 2 August 2026 dates if your go-live is before OJ publication; otherwise build for the Omnibus dates.
- **Article 50 is the closest new deadline.** With Article 50 enforcement landing on 2 December 2026 under the Omnibus and only 3 months of grace from OJ publication, transparency mechanisms (chatbot disclosure, machine-readable provenance signalling, deepfake labelling) need to be production-ready well in advance.
- **Update Acceptable Use Policies.** The new Article 5 prohibition on non-consensual intimate content and CSAM should be reflected in policies, vendor due diligence, and content-moderation classifiers immediately, even before formal adoption.
- **Track endorsement and OJ publication.** The earliest realistic OJ publication is summer 2026; any delay pushes Article 50's effective grace period later.
