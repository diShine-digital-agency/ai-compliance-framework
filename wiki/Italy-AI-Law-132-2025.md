# Italy National AI Law (Legge 132/2025)

Italy's **Legge 17 settembre 2025, n. 132 — Disposizioni e deleghe al Governo in materia di intelligenza artificiale** is the **first national AI law in the European Union**. It was approved definitively by the Italian Senate in September 2025 and entered into force on **10 October 2025**. It is designed to operate alongside (not in place of) the EU AI Act, with sector-specific rules for AI in healthcare, scientific research, employment, justice and public administration.

## Status and key dates

| Date | Event |
|------|-------|
| 17 September 2025 | Law 132/2025 approved definitively by the Italian Senate |
| **10 October 2025** | **Entry into force** |
| 12 March – 11 April 2026 | AgID **Determinazione 43/2026** (draft AI guidelines for public administration) public consultation |
| **10 October 2026** | Implementing decrees due (12 months from entry into force) |
| Throughout 2026 | Ministerial guidelines on medical-data processing for AI research expected from the Minister of Health |

## Designated authorities

Law 132/2025 designates the following bodies for AI Act implementation in Italy:

| Authority | Role |
|---|---|
| **AgID** (Agenzia per l'Italia Digitale) | National **notifying authority** under the EU AI Act; oversees Notified Bodies; issues guidelines for AI in public administration |
| **ACN** (Agenzia per la Cybersicurezza Nazionale) | National **market surveillance authority** for high-risk AI systems |
| **GPDP** (Garante per la Protezione dei Dati Personali) | Retains GDPR remit; joint competence on AI processing personal data |

## Substantive content

Key features of Legge 132/2025:

- **Sectoral focus.** Establishes domain-specific rules for AI in healthcare, scientific research, employment, justice and public administration, with emphasis on human oversight, transparency and accountability.
- **Compliance with the EU AI Act.** The law is explicitly aligned with Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 and does not derogate from it; it adds national-level operational detail and sanctioning powers.
- **Implementing decrees.** The Italian Government must adopt implementing decrees within 12 months of entry into force (**by 10 October 2026**) covering sanctioning powers, sectoral guidelines and operational details.
- **Healthcare research.** The Minister of Health is mandated to issue guidelines on the processing of medical data for AI research, expected during 2026 as part of the implementing-decree package.
- **Public administration.** AgID has issued draft *Linee guida per lo sviluppo di sistemi di IA nella PA* (Determinazione 43/2026), which were placed for public consultation between 12 March and 11 April 2026. Final guidelines will be issued as part of the implementing decrees.
- **Workplace AI.** The law sets specific informational and consultation duties for employers deploying AI that affects terms of work, performance evaluation, task assignment or selection. These duties operate in parallel with **Statuto dei Lavoratori (Law 300/1970) Article 4** (electronic monitoring requires prior agreement with workers' representatives or labour-authority authorisation).

## How Legge 132/2025 interacts with the EU AI Act and the Digital Omnibus

- The EU AI Act is directly applicable in Italy (it is a Regulation). Legge 132/2025 does not alter EU AI Act timelines.
- Where the Digital Omnibus on AI (provisional agreement of **7 May 2026**) shifts EU AI Act dates (high-risk to 2 December 2027, Article 50 to 2 December 2026, sandbox deadline to 2 August 2027), those shifts apply in Italy too once the Omnibus is published in the OJ.
- Until OJ publication, the original 2 August 2026 dates remain legally binding in Italy as elsewhere.

## Implications for Italian organisations

1. **Inventory and classify** all AI systems under the EU AI Act risk classification, then map sectoral rules under Legge 132/2025.
2. **Healthcare**: prepare for forthcoming Ministry of Health guidelines on medical-data processing for AI research; expect tighter conditions for special-category data under GDPR Article 9 in clinical research contexts.
3. **Public administration**: align procurement, FRIA processes and EU AI Database registration with both the EU AI Act and the AgID Determinazione 43/2026 framework once finalised.
4. **Workplace AI**: combine Statuto dei Lavoratori Article 4 informational/consultation duties with the Legge 132/2025 workplace-AI provisions; engage worker representatives early for any high-risk AI deployment affecting terms of work.
5. **Sanctions**: implementing decrees due by 10 October 2026 will define the sanctioning architecture; until then, EU AI Act penalties (Article 99) and GDPR penalties remain the primary enforcement risks.
